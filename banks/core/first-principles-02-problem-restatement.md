## Question

Break the stated problem down to facts that cannot be split further: who, at which step, blocked by what, how often, and what evidence (real cases or data, not impressions). Rebuild the problem from those facts. If the root cause lies elsewhere, would this requirement still exist once it is fixed? If the restated problem differs from the original, every later question applies to the restated one.

## Why it matters

A stated problem is often a symptom, or the mirror image of a preselected solution. Starting from the wrong problem makes every later question (size, deletion, safety) answer the wrong thing precisely, and the more rigorous the review, the more confident the error.
