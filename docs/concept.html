Shipright

AI drafts, humans sign, tools can only say no.

Humans are the bottleneck, so they only make the decisions that matter: what to build, approve the spec, approve the code, write the why.
Humans and AI supervise each other: the human reviews the AI's spec and code; the AI grills the human's plan before work starts.
The brain fills the gaps in human memory: decisions and their reasons live in a repo that outlasts any session. The thicker it gets, the fewer questions climb up to you.
Everything goes up as a PR, reviewed twice: first the spec, then the code.
Tools only have veto power. They are the edge of the shape, not a layer: they can refuse, never approve.
Human · decide AI judge · manage AI hands · execute Brain Tools (veto only)

1. Three layers, one spine

Borrowed from the classic decide / manage / execute split. Width is workload, and the shape is a funnel: problems enter at the bottom, the brain answers what it already knows, and only the rest climbs.

How to read it. The shape is the argument: it gets narrower going up, because humans are the bottleneck and should only decide what matters. The two boundary lines are where humans and AI check each other: the human reviews the AI's spec, and the AI grills the human's plan. The brain column on the right has three segments, and each layer reads and writes a different one of them. The dashed edges are tools: they can refuse, never approve, and they are not a layer.

2. Mechanism: tools, the PR, and two repos

The same three layers with the real pieces: a GitHub product repo where spec and code share one PR, a separate brain repo, and the grey chips where tools bite. Every chip can only refuse.

How to read it. The four bands map onto Figure 1: the human on top, the GitHub product repo where humans and AI hand work across, the AI (judge and hands) inside its fence, and the brain repo, which is the column from Figure 1 laid flat. The grey chips are tools, and each one can only refuse. Spec and code share one PR in the product repo, reviewed by a human twice. The brain is a separate repo, and decisions enter it only through a human: the AI drafts the what, and the human writes the why.
